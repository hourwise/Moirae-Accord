# ACCORD-01 Prior-Art Verification

## Scope and method

This report independently checks the load-bearing factual and prior-art claims in
the preserved Phase 1 and Phase 2 reviews. The review was bounded to the named
claims and to current public primary sources available on 2026-09-14. It does
not attempt to be a complete literature survey, and a source not found is not
treated as evidence that no such source exists.

The external review is evidence to evaluate, not an authority. Each entry below
separates what the reviewer said from what the independent check establishes.
ArXiv papers are treated as research preprints, Internet-Drafts as active
standardisation documents, and GitHub discussions/proposals as non-normative
unless the cited project identifies them as part of its current specification.

## Load-bearing claim verification

| ID | Claim | Review classification | Verification result | Source | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PA-01 | False success is empirically real in agent benchmarks. | The review cited arXiv 2606.09863 and reported 45–48% single-control false-success share of failures, 3% in dual-control telecom, 75.8% for AppWorld coding self-assessment, TF-IDF AUROC of 0.83/0.95, and roughly 3,300x lower latency. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The paper reports the stated benchmark populations and headline comparisons. The percentages are scoped to the paper's task/model/control configurations; they are not a universal rate for all agents or real-world effects. The paper's model-judge baselines and the TF-IDF baseline must not be conflated. | [Confident Closing to Silent Failure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09863) | Supports treating false confirmation as measurable, while requiring a pre-registered benchmark and careful population definitions. |
| PA-02 | Attenuating delegated authorization has substantial prior art. | The review named capability systems, Macaroons, Biscuit, UCAN, SPKI/SDSI, ZCAP-LD, and the OAuth attenuating-agent-token draft. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The general mechanism class is established. The current OAuth draft explicitly defines derived-token constraints, subset-style authority comparison, subsumption checks, offline chain verification, finite delegation depth, and bounded lifetime. The existence of those mechanisms does not establish that the exact agent task/effect problem, composition semantics, or implementation is solved. | [OAuth Attenuating Agent Tokens Internet-Draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-niyikiza-oauth-attenuating-agent-tokens/) | H-AUTH cannot be presented as an unexplored mechanism. Any novelty must be narrower than “child authority can be attenuated.” |
| PA-03 | The attenuating-agent-token work is a finished standard. | The review sometimes used the draft as evidence that the problem is already solved. | OVERSTATED. The Datatracker page identifies it as an Internet-Draft and working document, not a completed RFC or finished standard. The draft itself leaves individual derived-token revocation out of scope, discusses short lifetimes and stateful controls for side effects, and identifies formal verification as ongoing. | [IETF Datatracker status and draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-niyikiza-oauth-attenuating-agent-tokens/) | Prevents the response from replacing one overclaim with another. Active standardisation is evidence of prior art, not proof of a settled interoperable solution. |
| PA-04 | UNKNOWN/in-doubt transaction semantics are old distributed-systems prior art. | The review connected `UNKNOWN` to XA/two-phase commit, in-doubt outcomes, Two Generals, and FLP. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. Oracle documents transactions becoming in-doubt when two-phase commit is interrupted and requiring recovery or manual resolution. Microsoft documents an in-doubt state where the final outcome is not known. This establishes prior art for uncertainty and recovery obligations, but it does not prove exact equivalence to an agent effect-evidence taxonomy or to every proposed `UNKNOWN` state. | [Oracle: Managing Distributed Transactions](https://docs.oracle.com/en/database/oracle/oracle-database/19/admin/managing-distributed-transactions.html), [Microsoft: DTC transaction states](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/windows/desktop/ms681727%28v%3Dvs.85%29) | `UNKNOWN` is useful only if Accord states what remains unknown, what may reconcile it, and whether redispatch is allowed. |
| PA-05 | A2A has an unspecified/unknown task state. | The review cited `TASK_STATE_UNSPECIFIED` as related to in-doubt semantics. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The current A2A protocol defines `TASK_STATE_UNSPECIFIED` as an unknown or indeterminate task state. It is a task-lifecycle enum value, not by itself a distributed-transaction outcome, evidence receipt, or external-effect settlement. The protocol also defines `COMPLETED` as a task state; it does not thereby establish the external effect. | [A2A protocol definitions](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/definitions/), [A2A canonical proto](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/blob/main/specification/a2a.proto) | A2A can carry lifecycle information, but an Accord evidence model must not map A2A task completion directly to effect success. |
| PA-06 | Durable execution, retries, claims, and reconciliation are established engineering. | The review named Temporal, Restate, DBOS, Cadence, Step Functions, and Durable Functions. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. Temporal, Restate, DBOS, and AWS documentation all describe durable recovery, persisted workflow/step state, retries, and idempotency guidance. The sources also make the external-effect limit visible: retries can repeat an operation, and provider idempotency or an at-most-once/no-retry boundary is needed. Durable execution is not an arbitrary external API exactly-once guarantee. | [Temporal documentation](https://docs.temporal.io/), [Temporal architecture](https://github.com/temporalio/temporal/blob/main/docs/architecture/README.md), [Restate durable execution](https://restate.dev/what-is-durable-execution), [DBOS architecture](https://docs.dbos.dev/architecture), [AWS idempotency and retries](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/durable-execution/patterns/best-practices/idempotency/) | Horae-like machinery is not a sufficient novelty claim. The research must test what evidence and attribution add beyond a durable workflow plus provider-state read. |
| PA-07 | Durable workflow engines treat an activity return as external truth. | The review used this as a broad criticism of existing execution systems. | OVERSTATED. Durable systems record and replay workflow/activity results, but the cited documentation is more careful: external side effects may repeat, steps should be idempotent, and an idempotency key or provider-specific behavior is required. It is accurate to say that a workflow result is not automatically proof of an external effect; it is too broad to attribute one uniform “activity return equals truth” rule to all engines. | [AWS durable execution idempotency guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/durable-execution/patterns/best-practices/idempotency/), [Temporal architecture](https://github.com/temporalio/temporal/blob/main/docs/architecture/README.md) | The project should attack a precise baseline, not a caricature of durable execution. |
| PA-08 | Effect receipts and proof-carrying work have substantial adjacent prior art. | The review cited Notarized Agents, Proof-Carrying Agent Actions, RAILS, AP2, TessPay, and related receipt systems. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. Notarized Agents proposes receiver-attested receipts and a transparency log, while explicitly discussing suppression, collusion, and adoption limits. PCAA proposes runtime-neutral action certificates and outcome closure. RAILS proposes evidence envelopes and clearing decisions for agentic commerce. AP2 uses signed payment mandates and mandate verification. These sources establish a crowded adjacent field; they do not demonstrate that a general, transport-neutral, authority-to-effect evidence record with the proposed experiments already exists. | [Notarized Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04193), [Proof-Carrying Agent Actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04104), [RAILS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08790), [AP2 specification](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2) | “Portable evidence record” is not a blank-slate contribution. The differentiator, if any, must be an empirically tested composition and verifier boundary rather than the receipt label alone. |
| PA-09 | AP2 already solves delegated authority and effect proof generally. | The review treated AP2 as a nearby prior-art example. | OVERSTATED if read generally. AP2 provides a payment-specific mandate and verification model, including signed intent/checkout/payment artifacts. That is meaningful prior art for authorization and payment evidence, but it is not a general solution for arbitrary delegated work, arbitrary provider effects, effect attribution across transports, or task/effect reconciliation. | [AP2 agent authorization framework](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2), [AP2 mandates](https://ap2-protocol.org/specification/mandates/) | The experiment should include payment-like and non-payment-like surfaces or state why its scope is deliberately narrower. |
| PA-10 | Governance/control-plane designs are crowded and leave little differentiation. | The review cited PCAA, Five-Plane, Sovereign Execution Broker, Deterministic Control Plane, and similar work. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. PCAA alone demonstrates a current runtime-neutral governance/certificate design that overlaps the proposed control-plane vocabulary. The conclusion that there is “approximately zero differentiation” is an analytical judgment, not a verified fact. Differentiation remains an open empirical question and may disappear after comparison. | [PCAA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04104) | The project must compare against a no-new-runtime baseline and publish the boundary of any remaining contribution before implementation. |
| PA-11 | A2A itself provides delegation attenuation. | The review stated that current A2A provides task transport but not delegated authority attenuation. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The current A2A specification defines task/message/artifact lifecycle structures and an authentication-required state. Its authentication section states that A2A does not define the scope, representation, validity, or revocation semantics of an authorization credential; those are left to implementations. No native monotonic child-authority relation was found in the inspected current specification. This does not prohibit an extension or an external authority protocol. | [A2A canonical proto](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/blob/main/specification/a2a.proto), [A2A protocol definitions](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/definitions/) | A2A should be a conformance/interoperability target, not the source of Accord authority semantics. |
| PA-12 | A2A independently verifies external effects. | The review stated that A2A completion is an agent task state, not independent effect verification. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. A2A's current task model provides lifecycle/status and artifacts, but the specification does not make a general external observer, provider receipt, or independent effect verifier authoritative. A2A artifacts may carry evidence by extension; the protocol alone does not establish its trust root or effect attribution. | [A2A current specification](https://a2a-protocol.org/latest/specification/), [A2A canonical proto](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/blob/main/specification/a2a.proto) | Remote task status must remain an observation until a separately defined evidence rule settles an effect. |
| PA-13 | A2A SEP #1404 is an accepted standard feature. | The review referred to a proposal/discussion around delegated capability grants. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The item is a discussion/proposal surface, not evidence that the current A2A specification normatively includes the proposed capability-grant semantics. It is relevant prior art and a signal of active design work, but not a stable dependency or accepted standard. | [A2A discussion #1404](https://github.com/a2aproject/A2A/discussions/1404) | Accord must not anchor its research question to an unsettled A2A proposal. |
| PA-14 | The oracle/observability limit is technically relevant. | The review argued that no control plane can prove external truth without a trusted observation path. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The oracle framing is useful as a model boundary: an observer can only establish claims relative to accessible state, evidence semantics, and a trust root. The classic testing-oracle literature supports treating “does this observation satisfy the specification?” as a distinct problem. This is not an impossibility theorem for all effect verification; signed provider receipts, witnesses, and transparent logs can strengthen evidence without making it provider-independent truth. | [The Oracle Problem in software testing](https://academic.oup.com/comjnl/article-abstract/57/7/987/352048), [Notarized Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04193) | Future claims must name the observer, evidence class, read semantics, and trust root. |
| PA-15 | “Completion truth” is indefensible terminology. | The review rejected “truth” and unqualified “completion.” | CONFIRMED. Bare “truth” and “completion truth” combine a lifecycle label with an unbounded epistemic claim. The response adopts evidence-backed effect settlement and explicit evidence/observer terminology instead. | Local ACCORD-00 evidence plus PA-04, PA-06, PA-14 | Better terminology makes the claim testable and avoids implying that a control plane sees reality without an observation authority. |
| PA-16 | Effect attribution is distinct from effect observation. | The review separated occurrence, observation, attribution, and goal satisfaction. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The distinction follows directly from the receipt and idempotency sources: a system may observe provider state without proving which request caused it, or receive a signed receipt without proving that the requested goal was satisfied. The exact taxonomy remains a design question. | [AWS idempotency guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/durable-execution/patterns/best-practices/idempotency/), [Notarized Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04193), [PCAA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04104) | The experiment must score occurrence, attribution, and goal satisfaction separately. |
| PA-17 | The review’s proposed 20–30% abandonment/threshold rule is evidenced. | A numerical threshold was suggested as an example of a possible stop rule. | NOT_CONFIRMED. No cited source justifies that threshold for this project. It is not adopted. Thresholds must be selected after a pilot, then pre-registered before the research experiment. | No source identified; see PA-01 for benchmark-specific values only | Avoids manufacturing an abandonment rule from an unsupported number. |
| PA-18 | A depth-1, one-child, local synthetic smoke test cannot establish the joined research claim. | The review treated the supplied v0.1 boundary as insufficient research evidence. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. The local smoke test can establish wiring and bounded single-host engineering properties. It cannot test sibling composition, multi-hop attenuation, provider uncertainty, network loss, external evidence trust, or cross-transport generality. | ACCORD-00 [v0.1 boundary](../research/ACCORD-00-V0.1-BOUNDARY.md), [AWS idempotency guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/durable-execution/patterns/best-practices/idempotency/) | The engineering demo and the research experiment are now separate gates. |
| PA-19 | Firecracker or a sandbox is required for all prevention claims. | The review distinguished a mediated-channel guarantee from whole-process escalation prevention. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. A claim about every effect path available to an agent requires complete mediation or equivalent host enforcement. Firecracker is one possible enforcement mechanism, not a logical prerequisite for testing a mediated adapter/channel. Without complete mediation, Accord must not claim whole-process privilege-escalation prevention. | ACCORD-00 [Fates delta audit](../research/ACCORD-00-FATES-DELTA-AUDIT.md), ACCORD-00 [v0.1 boundary](../research/ACCORD-00-V0.1-BOUNDARY.md) | Firecracker remains deferred for the research/specification phase and is required only if the claim is widened to whole-process containment. |
| PA-20 | Ananke + Horae, with a portable record and verifier, may replace a new Accord runtime. | The review proposed a standalone record/verifier and direct use of existing authority and durable-execution owners. | CONFIRMED_WITH_QUALIFICATION. ACCORD-00 evidence supports Ananke as authority/policy owner and Horae as durable execution/effect-state owner. A portable record/verifier can be a specification and test instrument. Whether that combination is sufficient is an empirical architecture question; the new runtime/control-plane box is not justified merely by the existing diagram. | ACCORD-00 [reuse matrix](../research/ACCORD-00-REUSE-MATRIX.md), ACCORD-00 [Fates delta audit](../research/ACCORD-00-FATES-DELTA-AUDIT.md) | The binding decision rejects a new Accord runtime and preserves only a research/specification/profile role. |

## What the verification does not establish

- It does not establish that any cited paper, vendor, or protocol is equivalent
  to the proposed Accord record.
- It does not establish that signed provider evidence is provider-independent
  truth. It establishes a claim relative to a signing key and trust policy.
- It does not establish that a current A2A task state is an effect receipt.
- It does not establish a universal external-effect exactly-once guarantee.
- It does not establish that a formal authority relation for all resource,
  capability, deadline, rate, budget, approval, audience, and representation
  dimensions has a single shared attenuation algebra.
- It does not establish that the chosen research question is novel. Novelty is a
  question for the later research record and must be assessed against a broader,
  current search before publication claims.

## Verification conclusion

The external reviewer was right to force a split between authority attenuation
and effect-evidence hypotheses, to reject unqualified completion/truth language,
to require an external verifier, and to demand a stronger baseline and fault
matrix. The reviewer overstated the extent to which prior art already solves the
joined problem when it treated active drafts, adjacent receipts, and durable
execution products as a completed general solution. The resulting project is a
research/specification/verification effort with no new Accord runtime accepted
at this stage.
